Wall blocks

When someone speeds the ball up at you, a calm block wins more points than a big swing. Quiet hands come from repetition.

10 minutestime
A flat wall (a practice wall, garage or tennis backboard), one ballwhat you need

You've got it when

3 sets of 50 soft volleys.

Setup

Stand about 7 feet from the wall in a ready position, paddle out in front at chest height.

How to do it

  1. Hit soft volleys against the wall without letting the ball bounce.
  2. Keep the paddle out in front and use a short punch, not a swing.
  3. Count each set. Rest between sets.
  4. Mix forehand and backhand as you get comfortable.

Common mistakes

  • Letting the paddle drop between hits. Keep it up in front, ready.
  • Swinging from the shoulder. Keep the motion short, mostly from the elbow.

Make it easier

Let the ball bounce once between hits until you can keep it going.

Make it harder

Step in to 5 feet, or alternate forehand and backhand every hit.
